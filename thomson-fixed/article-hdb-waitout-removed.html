15-Month Wait-Out Period Removed — What This Means for Private Property Buyers

Published 28 July 2026 by Jet Lee, Senior Associate Group District Director, PropNex Realty

Breaking news: National Development Minister Chee Hong Tat announced the removal of the 15-month wait-out period for private property owners buying HDB resale flats, effective immediately. This marks a major policy shift that could bring a fresh wave of buyers into the private property market.

What Changed?

Previously, anyone who sold a private property had to wait 15 months before they could buy an HDB resale flat. This cooling measure was introduced in September 2022 during the pandemic to moderate demand for HDB resale flats amid supply shortages.

With immediate effect from 28 July 2026, this 15-month wait-out period has been removed entirely. Private property owners and former owners can now buy a non-subsidised HDB resale flat without the waiting period.

Source: Channel NewsAsia — 15-month wait-out period for private property owners to buy HDB resale flats removed: Chee Hong Tat

What Hasn't Changed?

It's important to understand what's still in place:

  • Private property owners must sell their existing unit within 6 months of completing the HDB resale flat purchase — whether in Singapore or overseas.
  • Subsidised flats still have a 30-month wait. If you want to buy a BTO flat (with or without grants), a resale flat with housing grants, or an EC from a developer, you must wait 30 months after selling your private property.
  • HDB loans are not available for those buying under the relaxed rules — this applies only to non-subsidised resale purchases.

Why Was It Removed Now?

The government assessed that the measure had "met its purpose." Key indicators:

  • HDB resale prices have declined for two consecutive quarters — down 0.1% in Q1 and 0.3% in Q2 2026
  • This follows five consecutive quarters of slower or no price growth from late 2024 through 2025
  • A larger number of flats completing their Minimum Occupation Period (MOP) are entering the resale market
  • Construction of BTO flats has largely recovered from pandemic disruptions

From the government's perspective, supply and demand have rebalanced enough that the temporary measure is no longer needed.

What This Means for Property Buyers and Sellers

💰 For HDB Upgraders (Selling HDB → Buying Condo)

This is the biggest group that benefits indirectly. Here's why:

When million-dollar HDB owners sell their flats, they now have more options for their next move. Before this change, many were hesitant to sell because downsizing back to HDB from private property was blocked by the 15-month wait. Now they can sell their HDB, buy a condo, and if needed later, buy back an HDB resale without penalty.

This removes a psychological barrier — more HDB owners will be willing to sell and upgrade to private property, knowing they have a clear exit path.

🏠 For Private Property Owners (Right-Sizing)

Singaporeans aged 55+ were already exempted for 4-room or smaller resale flats, but this change extends flexibility to all age groups. Empty nesters who want to unlock equity from their private property and move to a smaller HDB flat can now do so seamlessly.

📈 For the Condo Market

More HDB sellers entering the market means more potential buyers for new launch condos and resale private properties. The million-dollar HDB trend has created a pool of buyers with significant cash proceeds — and they're now more likely to deploy that capital into private property.

My Take — 18 Years in the Market

This is a significant policy shift that signals confidence in the market's stability. The government wouldn't remove a cooling measure unless they were comfortable with where prices are heading. For buyers who have been waiting on the sidelines — especially HDB upgraders and right-sizers — this is a clear green light to move forward.

If you've been considering upgrading from HDB to private property, the removal of this barrier removes much of the uncertainty about your future plans. You're no longer locked into a one-way decision.

With new launches like Thomson Reserve (D20, Upper Thomson), Loyang Valley Residences (D17, Loyang), and other upcoming projects, there's strong supply coming to market. But with more buyers now entering the pool, prices may not stay flat forever.
