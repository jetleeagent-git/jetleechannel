The 20% ABSD Trap — Why So Many Upgraders Miss Out on Their Dream Condo

Published 30 June 2026 by Jet Lee, Senior Associate Group District Director, PropNex Realty

Quick summary: This conversation has happened more times than I can count in my 18 years as a property agent. The names change, the numbers stay the same — and so does the regret. If you're planning to upgrade from HDB to condo, get your timeline and ABSD strategy clear before you fall in love with a unit.

A Conversation I Have All the Time

Buyer: "Jet, I'm planning to upgrade to a condo. Especially a new launch."

Jet: "Sure! By the way, do you currently have any property under your name?"

Buyer: "Yes, I own an HDB with my wife."

Jet: "I see. So if you're planning to buy a new launch condo while still holding your HDB, you'll need to pay 20% ABSD upfront. You can claim this back later once your HDB is sold — but only if you sell within the required timeframe. Alternatively, if timing allows, you could start selling your HDB now. If you manage to sell before you commit to the new launch, you avoid the extra 20% altogether."

Buyer: "Oh... I don't have that extra 20% ABSD cash ready. But let me just go see the showflat first. If I like it, I'll sell my HDB then buy."

And This Is Exactly Where the Regret Begins

By the time many buyers decide to sell, the unit they loved is already gone — snapped up by someone who was ready to commit on the spot. Worse, it's not just about missing one unit type or one stack. It's about missing the entire window.

The next launch they consider is often priced higher. The one after that, higher still. What started as "let me just go see first" turns into years of watching prices climb while they remain stuck on the sidelines, still trying to sell, still trying to get their finances in order.

The Two Real Paths Forward

If you own an HDB and want to buy a new launch condo, you have two practical paths:

  1. Sell first, buy after. No ABSD at all on the condo purchase since it becomes your first private property. The trade-off: you need somewhere to stay between selling and the new launch's completion, and you risk missing units if your sale takes longer than expected.
  2. Buy first, sell within 6 months. Pay 20% ABSD upfront on the condo, then apply for ABSD remission once your HDB sale completes within 6 months of exercising the Option to Purchase. This lets you secure your preferred unit without waiting — but requires the cash buffer to pay the ABSD upfront, even temporarily.

Both paths work. What doesn't work is going to view a showflat with no plan, falling in love with a unit, and only then trying to figure out the money and timeline. By then, the unit — or the entire launch — may already be gone.

The Lesson

If you already have a firm decision to upgrade — don't hesitate. Get your numbers and your timeline clear before you fall in love with a unit:

  • Do you have the cash to cover 20% ABSD upfront, or can you genuinely sell your HDB in time?
  • What's your realistic selling timeline based on current market conditions?
  • Are you prepared to commit the moment you find the right unit — or will you need to "go back and think"?

The market doesn't wait for buyers to get ready. It rewards those who already are.

If you're seriously considering an upgrade, the smartest first step isn't visiting a showflat — it's having an honest conversation about your numbers, your HDB's sale timeline, and your ABSD exposure. That conversation costs you nothing, and it could save you years of regret.
