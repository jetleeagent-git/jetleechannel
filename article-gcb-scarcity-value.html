Buying Beside Good Class Bungalows: Does It Increase Long-Term Value?

Published 15 July 2026 by Jet Lee, Property Journey Advisor

Quick summary: Good Class Bungalows are governed by some of Singapore's strictest land-use controls. With fewer than 2,800 GCBs across the entire island, their scarcity is unmatched. Properties located adjacent to GCB enclaves benefit from this scarcity premium, stable neighbourhoods, and long-term value preservation. Amberwood at Holland sits within the prestigious D10 GCB enclave.

What Makes a Property "Scarce"?

In real estate investment, scarcity is one of the most powerful drivers of long-term value. When something cannot be easily replicated, its value tends to hold — or increase — over time.

In Singapore, there are many forms of scarcity:

  • Freehold land in the Core Central Region
  • Land beside MRT stations
  • Units with unblocked city views

But Good Class Bungalows (GCBs) represent one of the rarest and most protected asset classes in Singapore property. And the scarcity of GCB land has a direct impact on the value of neighbouring properties.

The GCB Planning Controls

The Urban Redevelopment Authority (URA) imposes strict guidelines on all GCB areas:

  • Minimum land area: No less than 1,400 square metres (approximately 15,070 square feet)
  • Maximum height: Two storeys only
  • Building footprint: Maximum 35% of land area
  • Designated zones: GCBs are permitted only in 39 exclusive areas across Singapore

These controls ensure that GCB areas will never be redeveloped into high-density housing. The character, spaciousness, and exclusivity of these neighbourhoods are permanently protected.

Limited Supply: Only ~2,800 GCBs in All of Singapore

To put this in perspective:

  • There are approximately 2,800 Good Class Bungalows in Singapore
  • By contrast, there are over a million HDB flats
  • Private condominiums and apartments number in the hundreds of thousands

GCBs account for a tiny fraction of Singapore's total housing stock. And because the supply of GCB land is fixed — the URA does not designate new GCB areas — this scarcity is structural and permanent.

As Singapore's population of ultra-high-net-worth individuals grows, demand for GCBs continues to rise against a supply that barely moves. This creates a persistent scarcity premium that extends to properties in adjacent areas.

The Scarcity Premium for Adjacent Properties

When you purchase a condominium or apartment located beside a GCB enclave, you benefit from this scarcity effect indirectly:

1. Stable Neighbourhood Character

Unlike conventional landed housing areas, which may be sold for en-bloc redevelopment, GCB areas are permanently protected. Your views will not be blocked by a new 20-storey tower. The tree-lined streets and low-rise character will remain for decades. This certainty is rare in Singapore's fast-changing urban landscape.

2. Low Turnover, Long-Term Residents

GCB owners rarely sell. These are generational homes, passed down through families. The neighbourhood has a stable resident base of long-term owners who are invested in maintaining the area's character. This low turnover contributes to a stable, predictable environment that holds value well.

3. Prestige Address Premium

An address adjacent to a GCB enclave — particularly in D10 — carries a premium that is not easily replicated. Buyers are willing to pay more for the cachet of living in one of Singapore's most exclusive neighbourhoods, even if the specific development itself is not a GCB.

4. Natural Amenities and Green Buffer

GCB areas are among Singapore's greenest residential zones. They are defined by wide setbacks, mature trees, and proximity to park connectors and nature reserves. For residents of adjacent condominiums, this green buffer effectively extends your living environment.

What Investors Should Consider

For investors evaluating Amberwood at Holland, the GCB adjacency is a meaningful factor in long-term value preservation, but it should be considered alongside other fundamentals:

  • Entry price relative to future replacement cost — does the price reflect today's or tomorrow's value?
  • Location and accessibility — walking distance to King Albert Park MRT is a structural advantage
  • Supply constraints — limited land in the Holland Plain precinct supports value
  • School proximity — within 1km of MGS and near multiple elite schools
