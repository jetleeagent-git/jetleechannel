Cross Island Line Phase 3 Confirmed — 4 New MRT Stations, Interchanges at Jurong Pier & Gul Circle

Updated July 31, 2026 · by Jet Lee

On July 31, 2026, Transport Minister Jeffrey Siow announced the third and final phase of the Cross Island Line (CRL) — Singapore's eighth MRT line and, when complete, the country's longest fully underground rail line at over 65km from Changi in the east to Tuas in the west.

The final phase adds four new underground stations (CR20 to CR23) across roughly 10km, with two interchanges at Jurong Pier and Gul Circle. Work is slated to begin in 2027, with completion targeted in the late 2030s.

📌 Key Facts:

🚇 CR20 — serves Taman Jurong residents

🚇 CR21 — gateway to Jurong Island; links to the upcoming Jurong Region Line (JRL)

🚇 CR22 — located within Jurong Industrial Estate

🚇 CR23 (Gul Circle) — interchange with the East-West Line (EWL)

🏗️ Construction starts 2027 · Completion late 2030s

The Full Cross Island Line Picture

The CRL is being built in three phases:

  • Phase 1 (2030): 12 stations over 29km, from Aviation Park (Changi) to Bright Hill — including Loyang, Defu, and the Bright Hill interchange with the Thomson-East Coast Line.
  • Phase 2 (2032): 15km from Turf City to Jurong Lake District — including King Albert Park (Singapore's deepest station at ~50m), Maju, Clementi and West Coast. Interchanges with the Downtown Line at King Albert Park and the EWL at Clementi.
  • Phase 3 (late 2030s): ~10km from Jurong Lake District to Tuas — CR20 to CR23, with interchanges at Jurong Pier (JRL) and Gul Circle (EWL).

A 7.3km, four-station extension linking Pasir Ris and Punggol is expected to follow in 2032.

11 Interchanges Strengthen the Network

Minister Siow noted that the CRL will play a critical role in the rail network with 11 interchange stations. This strengthens network resilience — during a service disruption, commuters have alternative routes. When fully operational, the CRL is expected to serve at least 600,000 passenger trips daily, projected to grow to around one million in the longer term.

The first CRL train arrived in Singapore earlier in 2026. Commuters can expect 44 fully automated six-car trains with wider gangways and smart monitoring systems for early fault detection.

What This Means for Property Buyers

Confirmed rail infrastructure has historically been a strong catalyst for property value in Singapore — not just for the stations themselves, but for the corridors between them. Here's the project-by-project read:

📍 Western Corridor (Phase 2 & 3)

  • ELTA Clementi (D5) — Clementi MRT becomes a dual-line node (EWL + CRL by 2032), with West Coast station also on the line. ELTA sits minutes from Clementi and is one of the biggest beneficiaries of Phase 2 connectivity.
  • Lucerne Grand (D22, Lakeside) — Jurong Pier (CR21) links to the JRL, and the entire Jurong Lake District transformation accelerates with a direct line to Tuas and beyond. Phase 2 already puts Jurong Lake District on the CRL by 2032; Phase 3 completes the western spine.

📍 Eastern & North-Eastern Corridor (Phase 1)

  • Loyang Valley Residences (D17) — doorstep access to Loyang MRT (CR3) on Phase 1, opening ~2029-2030, with one-seat rides toward the city, west and north.
  • Hougang Central (D19) — Defu station (CR9) on Phase 1 puts Hougang on the cross-island spine, connecting to both the North-East Line and the new line.

📍 Central Corridor

  • Thomson Reserve (D20) — sits near Bright Hill (CR12), the Phase 1 terminus that interchanges with the Thomson-East Coast Line — a rare double-line advantage.

💡 The Investor's Takeaway: Buyers who enter before a station opens historically capture the "pre-MRT premium" — the uplift that happens as infrastructure moves from announcement to construction to opening. With Phase 3 announced today and Phase 2 already under construction, the western corridor (Clementi, Jurong) and Phase 1 corridor (Loyang, Hougang) are the zones to watch.

Frequently Asked Questions

When will CRL Phase 3 open?

Construction begins in 2027, with completion targeted in the late 2030s. Noise and vibration impact studies will be carried out before work starts.

Which properties will be acquired for Phase 3?

Seven industrial properties will be fully acquired and eight partially acquired (minor parts such as boundary fences). Affected businesses can continue operations; acquired parts are restored after works.

Is the CRL fully underground?

Yes — when completed, the CRL will be Singapore's longest fully underground rail line, spanning more than 65km.

How does this affect home prices?

Historically, properties near confirmed MRT stations see demand and price uplift as opening approaches. The "pre-MRT window" (announcement to opening) has generally been the more attractive entry point, though every project should be assessed on its own merits.
