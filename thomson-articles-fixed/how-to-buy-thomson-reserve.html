📖 Buyer's Guide

How to Buy Thomson Reserve — From Registration to Keys

Over 500 property transactions later, I can tell you: the buying process is where most people make costly mistakes. Here's exactly how it works for Thomson Reserve.

Published July 2026 · By Jet Lee, PropNex Realty (CEA Reg No. R007613B)

Buying a new launch condo in Singapore sounds simple — you register, you choose a unit, you sign. But the order of events, the timing of payments, and the strategy you use during balloting can make a real difference to which unit you end up with and how much it costs you.

Here's the complete step-by-step process for buying Thomson Reserve, based on how UOL/CapitaLand/SingLand have run their previous launches.

Step 1 · Now

Register Your Interest

Go to thomson-reserve-direct-developer.com and fill in the contact form. Or message me directly on WhatsApp. This puts you on the official database for:

  • Showflat appointment — VVIP preview slots are prioritised for early registrants
  • Price list — released 1–2 days before launch to registered buyers only
  • E-brochure & floor plans — as soon as they're confirmed by the developer
  • Balloting updates — you'll receive SMS/email with your queue number
💡 Insider Tip Register with an agent (me) who has a direct relationship with the developer. Agents with high sales volume get better VVIP slots for their clients. If you register directly or through a random agent, you may end up in the general public queue.
Step 2 · Before Preview

Secure Your Financing

Don't wait until you've selected a unit. Get your In-Principle Approval (IPA) from a bank before showflat day. Here's what you need:

  • MSR applies to HDB upgraders — if you're selling your HDB, know how much you can borrow
  • TDSR (Total Debt Servicing Ratio) — capped at 55% of your income for most borrowers
  • Bank IPA letter — valid for ~30 days; shows the developer you're a serious, ready buyer
  • Compare rates — fixed vs floating; 3 banks minimum
💡 Insider Tip Some buyers get their IPA only to find the bank values the property lower than the purchase price. Ask your mortgage broker or banker to run a stress test at 3.5% interest so you know your true ceiling.
Step 3 · Preview Day

Showflat Visit & Stack Selection

The showflat opens in phases. VVIP preview happens first (usually over 1–2 days), followed by public preview. Here's what you'll do:

  • View the showflat units (typically 2–3 layouts)
  • Review the official price list — released on the day
  • Compare stack options — facing, floor level, proximity to facilities
  • Shortlist your top 3–5 units in order of preference
💡 Insider Tip Don't get seduced by the showflat interior design. Focus on unit layout efficiency — walk-through, kitchen size, bedroom dimensions. Ask for the technical plan (the real architectural layout), not just the artist's rendering.
Step 4 · Balloting & Booking

Queue Number & Option Fee

Balloting determines who selects units first. Here's the sequence:

  • VIP ballot — high-value agents and their clients go first
  • General ballot — all other registered buyers, randomly drawn
  • Each buyer gets ~3–5 minutes to select and book
  • Option-to-Purchase (OTP) fee — typically 5% of purchase price, payable via cashier's order
💡 Insider Tip Have a backup stack ready. If your first choice is taken in the 30 seconds before your turn, you don't want to panic-pick. Know your #2 and #3 options so you make a calm decision under time pressure.
Step 5 · Within 8 Weeks

Sign the SPA (Sale & Purchase Agreement)

After booking, you have up to 8 weeks to sign the SPA. During this period:

  • Your lawyer reviews the SPA (developer's conveyancing)
  • You pay the balance downpayment — 20% total (5% OTP + 15% within 8 weeks)
  • Stamp duty (BSD/ABSD) is paid — your lawyer handles this
  • Loan documentation is signed with your bank
💡 Insider Tip Use a lawyer experienced with new launch conveyancing, not just resale. New launch SPAs have specific clauses about progressive payment schedules, TOP delays, and defect liability that a general property lawyer may miss.
Step 6 · Construction Phase

Progressive Payments (~2032 TOP)

Thomson Reserve's estimated TOP is ~2031–2032. During construction, you make progressive payments based on construction milestones:

  • Foundation work — ~10%
  • Reinforced concrete framework — ~40%
  • Partition walls, roofing, etc. — remaining portions
  • Total progressive payments follow the Normal Progressive Payment Scheme (NPS)
  • Interest on your loan accrues only on the disbursed amount, not the full purchase price
Step 7 · TOP & Keys

Collect Your Keys & Defect Check

When TOP is issued:

  • Developer notifies you to collect keys
  • You have 12 months from TOP to report defects
  • Engage a professional defect inspector — don't DIY this
  • Remaining 15–20% of purchase price is paid at TOP (or via loan drawdown)

💰 Quick Cost Summary for Thomson Reserve

  • OTP fee (booking): 5% of purchase price (cashier's order)
  • Balance downpayment: 15% within 8 weeks
  • BSD (Buyer's Stamp Duty): 1–6% tiered
  • ABSD: 0% (SC first property) / 20% (SC second property) / 60% (foreigner)
  • Legal fees: ~$3,000–$5,000
  • Agent commission: $0 (paid by developer)
  • Progressive payments: over construction period (~5 years)
  • Final payment at TOP: remaining ~15–20%

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Waiting to get financing after booking — get IPA first or risk losing your OTP fee
  • Registering with multiple agents — whoever registers you first gets the commission, and you get no benefit from doubling up
  • Skipping stack strategy — two units on the same floor can differ by $50K+ based on facing, pool view vs road noise
  • Not having a backup unit — balloting moves fast; hesitation costs you
  • Ignoring the fine print on progressive payments — know your cash flow obligations for the next 5 years
