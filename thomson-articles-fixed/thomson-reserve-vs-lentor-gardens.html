Thomson Reserve vs Lentor Gardens — Which 2026 Launch Is Better?

Two of the most talked-about 2026 new launches, but they couldn't be more different. Here's an honest side-by-side comparison to help you decide.

Published July 2026 · By Jet Lee, PropNex Realty (CEA Reg No. R007613B)

If you're shopping for a new launch condo in 2026, chances are you've come across both Thomson Reserve and Lentor Gardens. They're both big-name developments with strong developer backing, but they serve very different buyer profiles.

One sits in the RCR next to MacRitchie Reservoir with a TEL MRT at its doorstep. The other sits in the up-and-coming Lentor cluster with a more manageable size and an established developer group. Both have compelling cases.

Let's break down every angle — location, MRT, schools, pricing, and investment outlook — so you can decide which one fits your needs.

Location & Connectivity

Thomson Reserve sits in the Rest of Central Region (RCR) — District 20, Bright Hill Drive. It's directly beside Upper Thomson MRT on the Thomson-East Coast Line, and the upcoming Cross Island Line (CRL) interchange at Bright Hill will add another layer of connectivity. MacRitchie Reservoir and Windsor Nature Park are at your doorstep — this is a nature-front address that's hard to replicate.

Lentor Gardens is in the Outside Central Region (OCR) — District 26, Lentor. It's part of the growing Lentor master plan area, with Lentor MRT (TEL) within walking distance. The area is quieter and more suburban, with Hillock Park nearby. The Lentor cluster is still developing, so amenities will take a few more years to mature fully.

Winner: Thomson Reserve — RCR location with next-door TEL MRT and future CRL interchange.

School Priority

Thomson Reserve is within 1km of Ai Tong School — one of Singapore's most sought-after SAP primary schools. For families with young children, this is a structural advantage that both supports property value and gives P1 registration priority. This alone can justify a price premium over comparable projects.

Lentor Gardens is within 1km of Anderson Primary — a good school, but less competitive than Ai Tong. It's a solid option for families, but it doesn't carry the same premium factor.

Winner: Thomson Reserve — Ai Tong School 1km is a genuine differentiator.

Developer Reputation

Thomson Reserve's consortium — UOL Group, CapitaLand Development, and Singapore Land Group — is blue-chip. This same group delivered Skye At Holland, Parktown Residences, and Watten House. Track record is strong across luxury and mid-range segments.

Lentor Gardens brings Frasers Property, CSC Land, and Sekisui House — another excellent consortium. Frasers is well-respected, and Sekisui House is one of Japan's largest homebuilders. Their previous Lentor project was well-received.

Winner: Tie — both are top-tier developer groups.

Pricing & Value

Here's where things get interesting. Thomson Reserve's land cost of $1,178 psf/ppr is an RCR site priced below many OCR projects. For context, Chuan Grove (OCR) was acquired at $1,331 psf/ppr, and Bedok Rise (OCR) at $1,330 psf/ppr. Thomson Reserve's RCR location at OCR-equivalent land cost gives it a structural pricing advantage.

Lentor Gardens at ~$1,140 psf/ppr is competitively priced for OCR. The difference is marginal in land cost, but Thomson Reserve offers RCR location for only ~$38 psf/ppr more.

At launch, Thomson Reserve is expected to price around $2,000–2,300 psf, while Lentor Gardens may launch around $2,150+ psf. If pricing is as expected, Thomson Reserve offers better value for its location tier.

Winner: Thomson Reserve — RCR at OCR-equivalent cost.

Investment Outlook

Thomson Reserve has multiple value catalysts: the maturing TEL, the future CRL interchange at Bright Hill, Ai Tong School demand, and limited new launch supply in Upper Thomson. The land cost advantage also means the developer can price attractively. The main risk is the sheer number of units (1,268) creating resale competition at TOP.

Lentor Gardens benefits from the Lentor master plan — as the area develops, amenities improve and values grow. At 380 units, it's more manageable and faces less resale competition. However, the OCR location and lack of an elite school within 1km mean lower demand intensity from upgraders.

Winner: Thomson Reserve — stronger location fundamentals and more value catalysts over the long term.

🏆 Final Verdict

For families with young children: Thomson Reserve. The Ai Tong School 1km priority is hard to beat, plus the nature setting is unbeatable for raising kids.

For nature lovers and outdoor enthusiasts: Thomson Reserve. MacRitchie Reservoir at your doorstep — no other 2026 launch matches this.

For those who prefer a mid-sized community: Lentor Gardens. 380 units is more intimate than 1,268. Less crowd, more neighbourhood feel.

For pure investment: Thomson Reserve. RCR at OCR land cost + future CRL interchange + school demand driver = stronger appreciation catalysts.

Both are solid projects. But Thomson Reserve's combination of RCR location, school proximity, and land cost advantage gives it the edge for most buyer profiles.

Key Takeaways

  • Thomson Reserve — best for families with kids (Ai Tong 1km), nature lovers, long-term investors
  • Lentor Gardens — best for buyers who prefer a smaller, quieter OCR community
  • Land cost: Both competitive, but Thomson Reserve wins on value (RCR at near-OCR cost)
  • MRT: Thomson Reserve (next-door TEL + future CRL) beats Lentor Gardens
  • School: Thomson Reserve (Ai Tong SAP) beats Lentor Gardens (Anderson Primary)
  • Nature: Thomson Reserve (MacRitchie) beats Lentor Gardens (Hillock Park)
  • Resale competition: Lentor Gardens (380 units) is lower risk than Thomson Reserve (1,268 units)
