P1 Registration Phase 2C: Nearly Half of Primary Schools Oversubscribed — What It Means for Property Buyers

6 August 2026 · By Jet Lee

If you're a parent — or planning to become one near a popular primary school — this year's Primary 1 registration numbers deserve your attention.

According to data released by the Ministry of Education (MOE) on Wednesday (Aug 5), 79 of 179 primary schools accepting students in Phase 2C are oversubscribed. Of those with more applicants than vacancies, 73 will go to the ballot.

Phase 2C is the most competitive phase — it's open to all children, with 40 places reserved in each primary school. And for property buyers, it's a direct signal about which school zones carry the strongest demand.

The headline numbers:
  • 179 primary schools accepted students in Phase 2C
  • 79 oversubscribed — nearly half
  • 73 schools going to ballot
  • Most oversubscribed: Princess Elizabeth Primary (Bukit Batok) — 159 applicants for 40 places
  • Runner-up: Northland Primary (Yishun) — 141 applicants for 42 places

The Most Oversubscribed Schools

Princess Elizabeth Primary School in Bukit Batok topped the list with 159 students vying for 40 slots. Northland Primary School in Yishun followed with 141 applicants for 42 places. Nan Hua Primary, Chongfu School and Rosyth School completed the top five.

Notably, Princess Elizabeth, Northland, Nan Hua and Chongfu were also among the top five last year — these are persistent demand hotspots, not one-off spikes.

How Balloting Works

Balloting happens when applicants exceed vacancies. Priority admission order:

  1. Singaporeans living within 1km of the school
  2. Singaporeans living between 1km and 2km
  3. Singaporeans living further than 2km
  4. Permanent residents in the same distance groups (1km, 1–2km, beyond 2km)

The 1km advantage is decisive — that's why "within 1km of a popular school" has historically commanded a premium in Singapore property. This year's data confirms the pattern hasn't changed.

Bigger Picture: Fewer Spots Overall

There's another layer to this story. MOE earlier cut the Primary 1 intake for most schools this year in view of significantly falling student cohort sizes in 2027 and the coming years.

  • Compared to 2025, there are 1,460 fewer spots overall in 2026
  • Of 179 primary schools, 61 cut their intake while 12 increased spaces
  • The ministry said cuts would minimise the need for school mergers or relocations

Smaller cohorts mean fewer places — even as demand for popular schools stays intense. The net effect: oversubscription pressure concentrates in the schools parents most want.

What This Means for Property Buyers

1. The 1km premium is alive and well. When a school is oversubscribed and goes to ballot, Singapore Citizen children within 1km get first priority. For families serious about a specific school, living within 1km removes the biggest uncertainty in the entire registration process. That's why proximity to in-demand schools remains one of the most durable value drivers in Singapore property.

2. Watch the persistent hotspots. Schools like Princess Elizabeth, Northland, Nan Hua and Chongfu have been oversubscribed year after year. If you're buying near a school with sustained oversubscription, the demand isn't a fluke — it's structural.

3. New launches near strong school belts benefit. For new launch buyers, projects within 1km of popular primary schools have a measurable advantage — not just for registration priority, but for resale demand down the line when families with school-aged children look for homes.

4. The next phase matters too. Phase 2C Supplementary (for children who haven't registered yet) opens after this — vacancies will be updated by 1pm on Aug 11. If you're still deciding, that's the next milestone to watch.
