COV is Back in Singapore HDB Resale — What Buyers Need to Know (2026)

Published 20 June 2026 by Jet Lee, Senior Associate Group District Director, PropNex Realty

Quick summary: Cash-Over-Valuation (COV) has returned to Singapore's HDB resale market for the first time since 2014. When buyers agree to pay above HDB's official valuation, the difference must be paid in cash — CPF and loans cannot cover it. This article explains what COV is, why it's back, and what to watch out for.

What is COV?

COV stands for Cash-Over-Valuation. It is the difference between the agreed purchase price of an HDB resale flat and the official market valuation assigned by HDB.

For example, if you agree to buy a flat for $650,000 and HDB values it at $600,000, the COV is $50,000. This amount must be paid entirely in cash — you cannot use CPF savings or a bank loan to cover it.

Key point: COV is paid in cash only. It sits on top of your 5% cash deposit, stamp duties, and any shortfall between your loan and the purchase price. Always factor this into your budget.

Why Was COV "Gone" Until Now?

Before March 2014, HDB valuations were issued before buyers made offers. Agents would openly advertise flats with a stated COV — for example, "valued at $500k, asking $550k ($50k COV)." This made COV a normalised, negotiated number.

In March 2014, HDB changed the process. The sequence was reversed: buyers and sellers agree on a price first, then HDB conducts the valuation. Without the ability to advertise or negotiate COV openly, the market default shifted toward transacting near valuation. For nearly a decade, most HDB resale transactions had little to no COV.

Source: HDB — Resale Flat Valuation Process (hdb.gov.sg)

When prices rise quickly, HDB's valuations — based on a trailing window of recent transactions — can lag behind the current market. In popular estates where demand is strong, buyers may need to offer above valuation to secure a flat. The gap between market price and HDB valuation is what we now call COV.

How COV Affects Your Cash Budget

If you are buying an HDB resale flat in 2026, here is what your cash outlay may look like:

  • 5% cash deposit of the purchase price (1% option fee + 4% exercise fee)
  • COV — the excess over valuation, payable in cash
  • Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD) — calculated on purchase price, payable via CPF or cash
  • Legal fees — typically $2,000–$3,000

In HDB's Q1 2026 release, the agency noted that the RPI declined 0.1% quarter-on-quarter to 203.4 — the first quarterly decline since Q2 2019. This suggests that while prices remain elevated, the pace of growth is slowing.

Source: HDB — 1st Quarter 2026 Public Housing Data (hdb.gov.sg)

What to Do as a Buyer

  • Get a valuation estimate early. Review recent transacted prices for similar flats in the same block or nearby blocks on HDB's portal before making an offer.
  • Budget for COV. If you are looking in a popular mature estate, set aside additional cash beyond your 5% deposit in case the valuation comes in lower than the agreed price.
  • Negotiate. Not every seller can command a large COV. Use recent transaction data to support your offer.
  • Know your limits. If the COV is too high, you can walk away — you only lose the $1,000 option fee.
