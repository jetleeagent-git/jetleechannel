‘No Longer a Rising Tide’: Upcoming Q4 Launches Will Test Singapore Homebuying Demand

Published 07 September 2026 · By Jet Lee · Market Analysis · Source: The Business Times (Jeanne Mah)

While recent fervent buying in Singapore's residential market appears to have lost some steam following a dramatic rebound in 2025, industry analysts emphasize that underlying demand has not fundamentally weakened. Instead, homebuyers have become increasingly price-sensitive and selective as the market digests a long pipeline of new projects launched over the past two years.

“Developers are no longer selling into a rising tide in which every launch benefits equally. Every project now has to earn demand.” — Marcus Chu, CEO of ERA Singapore

As we enter the fourth quarter of 2026, a major line-up of new project launches will serve as a definitive litmus test for pricing strategies, buyer demand, and overall market sentiment across Singapore.

Major Q4 2026 Launches Testing the Waters

A quieter launch calendar in early 2026 led to lower overall new home sales, but Q4 brings several highly anticipated projects into the spotlight:

  • Thomson Reserve (Upper Thomson Road): A massive 1,268-unit development jointly developed by CapitaLand Development (CLD), UOL Group, and SingLand, scheduled to launch previews in October 2026.
  • Lucerne Grand (Lakeside Drive, Jurong): A 570-unit project by City Developments Limited (CDL) situated in the heart of Jurong Lake District.
  • Amberwood (Holland Road): A 212-unit residential development by Sim Lian, commencing previews on September 11, 2026.
  • The Serra Residences (Novena): A premier 133-unit freehold project along Bassein Road by Far East Organization.
Return to Market Fundamentals: Rather than signaling a shift in total structural demand, analysts agree that current conditions represent a return to fundamental value evaluation. Buyers now scrutinize location advantages, transport connectivity, layout efficiency, and competitive pricing far more rigorously than during previous market surges.

2026 Sales Data: Demand Remains Steady Against Controlled Supply

According to figures from the Urban Redevelopment Authority (URA) and Savills Singapore, developers sold 4,885 new private homes (excluding Executive Condominiums) in the first seven months of 2026 — an 11.6% decrease from 5,527 units in the same period last year.

However, new unit launches fell even more steeply, declining 28.7% to 4,516 units (down from 6,334 units in Jan–Jul 2025). As PropNex CEO Kelvin Fong highlighted, developer sales actually outpacing launches by 8.2% in 2026 indicates that buyer demand remains healthy relative to available new inventory.

Alan Cheong, Executive Director of Research & Consultancy at Savills Singapore, observed that the sales-to-launch ratio rose to 1.08 in 2026, surpassing the 5-year historical average of 1.05, confirming that transaction volumes have stayed broadly steady relative to supply.

A Tale of Two Launches: Why Project Characteristics Matter

Recent project performances demonstrate how selective buyers have become. Cushman & Wakefield's Wong Xian Yang pointed out that two projects launched at similar price points yielded vastly different initial take-up rates:

  • Tengah Garden Residences: Moved 99% of its 863 units during its April launch at an average of S$2,120 psf. CBRE noted that it benefited from first-mover advantage, direct connection to an upcoming MRT station, and integrated retail space.
  • Narra Residences: Achieved a 25% take-up rate at its January launch at S$2,180 psf, facing stiffer buyer resistance due to a longer walk to MRT facilities in Dairy Farm.

Linda Chern, Head of Residential Services at CBRE, characterized this moderation as a "temporary pause" as buyers build up capital reserves for major upcoming launches in the pipeline.

The S$2.5 Million Affordability Sweet Spot

Pricing strategy remains paramount for developers entering Q4. SRI Head of Research Mohan Sandrasegeran noted that homes priced at S$2 million and above accounted for 61% of non-landed private home sales in the first seven months of 2026 (up from 53.9% in 2025).

ERA's Marcus Chu identified S$2.5 million as a key psychological affordability threshold for buyers. Units priced below S$2.5 million consistently formed between 41% and 74% of monthly new private sales, proving to be the most resilient segment of buyer demand.

Full-Year 2026 Market Outlook & Projections

Leading agency heads and real estate research houses maintain a steady forecast for full-year 2026:

  • PropNex: Forecasts approximately 9,000 new private home sales in 2026.
  • ERA Singapore: Expects total new sales between 8,000 and 9,000 units.
  • CBRE: Projects between 7,500 and 8,500 sales, with full-year price growth moderating between 2% and 4%.

Additionally, secondary resale activity accounted for 62% of total private residential transactions in Q2 2026 (up from 52% in Q3 2025), showing that buyers are actively comparing new launch prices against high-value resale alternatives.

Jet Lee's Commentary: What Buyers Should Focus On in Q4

Navigating a Selective Market: In a "rising tide" market, buyers can sometimes afford to pick almost any unit and expect rising prices to lift their investment. In today's market, selectivity is everything. When evaluating Q4 launches like Thomson Reserve, Lucerne Grand, Amberwood, or The Serra, focus on three core pillars:
  1. Pricing relative to regional resale benchmarks: Ensure the launch psf leaves margin for growth compared to nearby resale transactions.
  2. Uncompromising location perks: Prioritize direct MRT connectivity, integrated amenities, or primary school proximity (within 1km).
  3. quantum vs psf: Watch out for the S$2.5M quantum threshold for 2-bedroom and 3-bedroom units to preserve liquidity for future resale exit.
