Why Wealthy Families Don't Chase the Tallest Condo

Published 15 July 2026 by Jet Lee, Property Journey Advisor

Quick summary: There is a common misconception that wealthy buyers gravitate toward the tallest, most prominent condominium towers. In reality, many affluent families prioritise privacy, space, low-density living, and neighbourhood character over height and flashy amenities. Amberwood at Holland embodies this philosophy.

The Psychology of Wealth in Property

In Singapore's property market, the most talked-about developments are often the tallest ones. The ones with 50-storey towers, endless facilities, and sprawling layouts that dominate the skyline.

But here is something you might not expect:

Many of the most discerning buyers — particularly families with significant wealth — actively avoid these developments.

Why? Because the priorities that define how ultra-high-net-worth families choose a home are fundamentally different from what the mass market assumes.

Privacy Is the Ultimate Premium

For wealthy families, privacy is not a luxury feature. It is a non-negotiable requirement.

In a 500-unit condominium with shared facilities, every trip to the pool, gym, or function room involves interacting with dozens of other residents. For high-profile individuals, business owners, and families who value discretion, this is not appealing.

Privacy in property means:

  • Fewer neighbours — less foot traffic, less noise, fewer shared amenities
  • Low-density living — developments with fewer than 250 units offer a more intimate environment
  • Spacious layouts — generous floor plans that allow family members to have their own space
  • Setbacks and greenery — visual and physical separation from neighbouring buildings

Low-Rise Living Appeals to the Discerning

There is a growing preference among affluent buyers for low-rise developments, particularly those situated within or near landed enclaves.

A low-rise development offers:

  • Ground-level connection — easier access to outdoor spaces, gardens, and nature
  • Reduced waiting times — no queuing for lifts in 40-storey towers
  • Better community feel — the scale is human, not institutional
  • Enhanced safety for children — parents feel more at ease when kids play in low-rise settings

Larger Homes in Landed Enclaves

Another key factor is the availability of genuinely large family-sized homes. Many modern high-rise condos prioritise efficient layouts that pack as many units as possible into the available space. The result is often compact bedrooms and living areas that feel cramped.

Wealthy families prioritise:

  • Generous square footage — true family living spaces, not shoe-box layouts
  • Quality over quantity of rooms — better to have 3 spacious bedrooms than 5 tiny ones
  • Functional layouts — homes designed for how families actually live

The GCB Environment Advantage

Nothing captures this philosophy better than living within or beside a Good Class Bungalow enclave.

GCB areas are defined by:

  • Minimum 1,400 sqm land plots — ensuring space and separation between homes
  • Maximum 2 storeys — maintaining a low-rise, open character
  • Strict planning controls — the neighbourhood will not change character
  • Tree-lined streets — natural greenery, not concrete canyons
Amberwood at Holland sits at the edge of the prestigious Old Holland and Brizay Park GCB enclave. At just 212 units with a low-rise profile, it offers the privacy, space, and neighbourhood character that wealthy families seek — without requiring the multi-million-dollar capital of a GCB.

What About Amenities?

For most buyers, a long list of amenities is a selling point. For wealthy families, it often means the opposite.

Consider this: If a development promotes "50 resort-style facilities," who will be using them? Hundreds of residents, all competing for the same spaces. Wealthy families who can afford private memberships at exclusive country clubs do not need a crowded function room or a shared BBQ pit in their condominium.

What they need is a home that serves as a sanctuary. And that requires space, privacy, and a serene environment — not another swimming pool.

The Flashy Development Trap

This is not to say tall condos are bad investments. Many perform well. But they cater to a different buyer profile.

The trap is assuming that "luxury" means the tallest, shiniest, most amenity-packed development. For buyers who value substance over show, the opposite is often true.

A common mistake among buyers: Chasing the tallest, flashiest development because it feels like "the best" — when what they actually need is a home that offers privacy, space, and a stable neighbourhood. Knowing which camp you belong to is half the battle.

My Personal View

I have accompanied countless families on their property journeys, and the pattern is clear. The most satisfied homebuyers — the ones who stay in their homes for years and watch their investment grow — did not choose based on the building's height. They chose based on how the home and neighbourhood made them feel.

Amberwood at Holland appeals to buyers who understand this distinction. It is not trying to be the tallest or flashiest development. It is designed for families who value substance, privacy, and the quiet confidence that comes with a well-chosen address.
