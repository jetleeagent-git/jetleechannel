Is Being Within 1km of a Popular Primary School Overrated? Here's What Really Drives Property Prices in Singapore.

Published 8 July 2026 by Jet Lee, Property Journey Advisor

Quick summary: Being within 1km of a popular primary school matters — but only if you have school-going children. For investors, upgraders, and long-term buyers, paying a $300k–$500k school premium may not make financial sense. The real drivers of property value go far beyond one criterion.

One of the first questions many buyers ask when considering a new condominium is:

"Is it within 1km of a popular primary school?"

For years, this has been one of the biggest selling points in Singapore's property market.

But here's a question few people ask:

Should you pay an extra $300,000 to $500,000 simply to be within 1km of a school?

The answer depends entirely on who you are buying the property for.

If You Have Young Children, It Matters.

Let's be fair.

If your child is entering Primary One and you are targeting a specific school, then being within the priority enrolment distance can be a genuine advantage.

For these families, buying within the preferred distance may provide convenience and improve the chances of securing a place, although school admission depends on the Ministry of Education's registration framework and cannot be guaranteed by buying a home alone.

If this is your primary objective, then paying some premium may be worthwhile.

But What If You Don't Have School-Going Children?

This is where many buyers make an expensive mistake.

Imagine paying an additional $400,000 simply because a condominium is within 1km of a school.

If:

  • Your children have already graduated,
  • You don't have children,
  • You are buying purely for investment,
  • Or you intend to sell before your future children enter Primary One,

Then are you really benefiting from that premium?

In many cases, the answer is probably not.

You may be paying for a feature that someone else values more than you do.

Good investing starts with paying for what creates value for your own goals, not simply following what everyone else is chasing.

Property Prices Are Driven by More Than Schools

Schools are only one piece of the puzzle.

Long-term capital appreciation is usually supported by multiple factors working together, including:

  • Transport connectivity
  • Government infrastructure investment
  • Future transformation
  • Employment hubs
  • Lifestyle amenities
  • Limited housing supply
  • Entry price
  • Future replacement cost
  • Buyer demand

A great property is rarely great because of one single reason. It is the combination of multiple strengths that creates lasting value.

The Danger of Buying Based on a Single Factor

One of the biggest mistakes buyers make is choosing a property because of only one selling point.

Examples include:

  • "I only buy freehold."
  • "It must be within 1km of a school."
  • "It must have a sea view."
  • "It must be the cheapest."

While each of these factors has merit, none of them alone determines whether a property is a good investment.

The strongest investments usually succeed because several positive fundamentals come together.

My SRPU Perspective

Whenever I analyse a property, I don't ask only one question. I evaluate it using my SRPU Framework:

S – Safe Entry

Am I buying at a reasonable entry price relative to future replacement cost?

R – Risk

What could limit future performance?

P – Protection

What long-term structural drivers could support future appreciation?

Rather than asking, "Is there a school within 1km?" perhaps the better question is:

"Will this location become a more valuable place to live over the next 10 to 15 years?"

Sometimes, asking the right question leads to a better investment decision.
