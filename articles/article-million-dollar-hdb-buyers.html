Who Buys Million-Dollar HDB Flats? New Survey Reveals the Typical Buyer

← Back to all articles

Published 3 August 2026 by Jet Lee, Senior Associate Group District Director, PropNex Realty

Quick take: A PropNex survey of 110 million-dollar HDB resale deals from 2025 found that 71% of buyers were aged 30-49, roughly 7 in 10 were PMET professionals, and the largest income band earned $10,001-$16,000 a month. Surprisingly, four-room flats — not five-room — now lead million-dollar transactions at 42.7% of deals.
Source: AsiaOne / PropNex — A new survey reveals the typical million-dollar HDB buyer in Singapore — including a surprising buying trend

The Million-Dollar HDB Market Keeps Growing

We've all seen the headlines about million-dollar flats — but until now, almost nothing was known about who is actually buying them and why.

PropNex decided to find out. The agency surveyed its own salespersons who closed million-dollar HDB resale transactions in 2025, gathering buyer profiles from 110 such deals. PropNex CEO Kelvin Fong is quick to caution that this is a modest base that the agency hopes to build on — but it still offers a rare, ground-level look at this growing group of buyers.

The numbers behind the trend are striking. From 1 January to 16 July 2026, at least 1,002 resale flats transacted for $1 million or more, according to transaction data. We are on track to meet or exceed the record 1,593 million-dollar flat sales recorded across the whole of 2025.

Who Is Buying? The Typical Profile

  • Age: 71% of transactions involved buyers aged 30-49, split between the 30-39 and 40-49 groups
  • Occupation: About 7 in 10 buyers were white-collar professionals in PMET (professional, managerial, executive, technical) roles
  • Household income: The largest single band (35.5%) earned $10,001-$16,000 a month; another 20.0% earned $16,001-$20,000; 3.6% earned $20,001-$25,000; and 2.7% earned above $25,000. On the lower end, 11.8% earned $5,000-$10,000 and just 0.9% below $5,000

PropNex notes that the income figures should be treated as indicative, since agents were sometimes unsure of a buyer's household income or the buyer chose not to disclose it.

The Retirement and Downsizing Story

Not all million-dollar buyers are young professionals. Buyers aged 50-59 who bought as retirement homes made up 24.5% of transactions. Of these, 30.8% had downsized from a private home, while 15.4% had relocated from a larger HDB flat.

For buyers aged 60 and above (mostly retirees), the story is even clearer: 71.4% downsized from a private home before buying a million-dollar HDB resale flat as their replacement home. This group typically bought four-room flats priced between $1.0 million and $1.328 million.

The Surprise: Four-Room Flats Lead Million-Dollar Deals

Here's the finding that upends conventional wisdom. Four-room resale flats were the most popular type of million-dollar transaction, accounting for 42.7% of deals — ahead of five-room flats at 35.5%, executive flats at 20%, and jumbo flats at 1.8%.

Anecdotally, it was assumed that five-room flats dominated million-dollar sales, given their larger size and relative scarcity. The survey shows that assumption was wrong — and it's a meaningful signal about how buyers are prioritising location and convenience over sheer size.

As Wong Siew Ying, Head of Research and Content at PropNex, puts it: flats — even million-dollar flats — serve different purposes at different life stages. "For younger buyers, a million-dollar resale flat may be an aspirational first home. For mid-career families, it could be an upgrading pathway that stays within the public housing segment." For older buyers, she notes, it's often a right-sizing move that trades unit size for location and convenience.

Where the Buyers Are Looking

  • First-time buyers (30-39): Typically bought four-room, five-room, executive, and jumbo flats in centrally-located mature estates — Bukit Merah, Kallang Whampoa, Queenstown, and Toa Payoh — priced from $1 million to $1.659 million
  • Upgraders (40-49): Split between OCR and RCR locations including Bishan, Bukit Merah, Clementi, Geylang, Serangoon, Tampines, and Toa Payoh, buying five-room and executive flats priced between $1.0 million and $1.38 million

Million-dollar sales facilitated by PropNex agents last year included units at Alkaff Courtview (Bidadari Park Drive), Clementi Crest, Park Central (Ang Mo Kio St 22), The Peak @ Toa Payoh, Telok Blangah ParcView, Havelock View (Alexandra), The Pinnacle @ Duxton, City View @ Boon Keng, St George's Towers (off Serangoon Rd), SkyTerrace @ Dawson (Queenstown), and Ghim Moh Edge.

COV Is the Exception, Not the Rule

Another reassuring finding: about 69.1% of buyers paid no Cash-Over-Valuation (COV) at all. Among those who did, 11.8% paid COV below $40,000, 6.4% paid between $40,000 and under $80,000, 3.6% paid $80,000-$100,000, and only 5.5% paid more than $100,000.

With most transactions completing without COV, this suggests current million-dollar HDB resale prices are supported by official valuations rather than bidding frenzies — a healthier picture than the headlines suggest.

What Actually Pulls Buyers In

When asked what drew them to their purchase, the top factors were interest in the town or neighbourhood, proximity to an MRT station, and a high floor.

Interestingly, the least instrumental factors were proximity to good schools, the physical condition of the flat, and being priced out of private homes in the same locale. For anyone assuming "good school within 1km" is the main driver of million-dollar prices — the survey says otherwise.

My Take

This survey matters because it destroys a few lazy assumptions. Million-dollar HDB buyers aren't one type — they're young PMET professionals buying aspirational first homes in mature central estates, AND retirees downsizing from private property to free up cash while staying close to amenities. The four-room finding is the most interesting: it tells me buyers are increasingly paying for location and lifestyle, not just size.

For sellers, the takeaway is to price based on comparable valuations in your specific town, not on the five-room narrative. For buyers, the fact that ~69% of these deals went through without COV is a useful negotiating data point — a strong valuation support matters more than the sticker shock of the $1 million headline.

With over 1,000 million-dollar transactions already logged by mid-July 2026 and the removal of the 15-month wait-out period bringing more upgraders into the market, this segment will only grow. Million-dollar flats may still be an outlier in the broader HDB market — but they are increasingly their own distinct tier.

Thinking of Buying or Selling an HDB Flat?

Whether you're eyeing a mature estate resale flat, planning to upgrade, or thinking about right-sizing from a private home, understanding what similar buyers are actually paying (and why) makes a real difference to your negotiation.
