Singapore Home Loan Guide 2026 — Fixed vs Floating Rates Explained

Published 22 June 2026 by Jet Lee, Senior Associate Group District Director, PropNex Realty

Quick summary: Choosing between a fixed-rate and floating-rate home loan is one of the most important financial decisions a property buyer makes. This guide explains the differences between HDB concessionary loans and bank loans, how fixed and SORA-pegged floating rates work, and what mortgage servicing rules apply.

Types of Home Loans Available

In Singapore, homebuyers can choose between two main loan types: HDB Concessionary Loans (for HDB flat buyers) and Bank Loans (for both HDB and private properties). Each has different interest structures, loan limits, and eligibility criteria.

Key point: The HDB loan rate is pegged to the CPF Ordinary Account rate + 0.1%, making it predictable and not subject to market fluctuations. However, you can only take an HDB loan if you don't own any other property.

Fixed vs Floating Rate Packages

Fixed Rate Packages

Fixed rates lock in your interest for a set period (typically 2 or 3 years). After the lock-in period, the rate typically converts to a floating rate.

✅ Predictable monthly payments ✅ Peace of mind during rate uncertainty ❌ Typically higher than initial floating rates ❌ Early repayment penalty during lock-in

Best for: Buyers with tight cashflow who prioritise payment certainty over potential savings.

Floating Rate (SORA-Pegged)

Most floating packages today track the Singapore Overnight Rate Average (SORA). The rate comprises a fixed spread + the prevailing SORA rate, which fluctuates with market conditions.

✅ Lower initial rates typically ✅ No lock-in (refinance flexibility) ❌ Monthly payments can rise ❌ Exposure to rate fluctuations

Best for: Buyers who have cashflow buffer and expect rates to stay low or decline.

Mortgage Servicing Rules You Need to Know

For HDB Flats

  • MSR (Mortgage Servicing Ratio): Monthly loan instalment cannot exceed 30% of your gross monthly income
  • LTV (Loan-to-Value) Limit: Up to 85% for HDB loans; up to 75% for bank loans

For Private Properties

  • TDSR (Total Debt Servicing Ratio): All monthly debt obligations cannot exceed 55% of your gross monthly income
  • LTV Limit: 75% for first property (lower for second property)

Source: Monetary Authority of Singapore — Property Cooling Measures (mas.gov.sg)

How Much Can You Borrow?

Your maximum loan amount depends on your income, age, existing debt obligations, and the loan type:

  • For HDB loans: Capped by MSR at 30% of gross monthly income
  • For bank loans: Capped by TDSR at 55% of gross monthly income (all debts included)

Should You Refinance?

If you're on an older floating package, it's worth checking whether refinancing could lower your monthly payments. Here's the checklist:

  1. Check your lock-in period end date
  2. Compare current rates from at least 3 banks
  3. Factor in legal and valuation fees
  4. Calculate the break-even point — how many months to recoup refinancing costs
