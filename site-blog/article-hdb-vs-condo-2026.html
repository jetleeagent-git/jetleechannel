HDB vs Condo 2026 — A First-Timer's Guide to Choosing Your First Home

Published 22 June 2026 by Jet Lee, Senior Associate Group District Director, PropNex Realty

Quick summary: The HDB vs condo decision is one of the biggest choices first-time homebuyers face in Singapore. HDB flats offer affordability and grants, while condos provide lifestyle and stronger long-term appreciation. This guide breaks down the pros, cons, and key differences in the 2026 market.

1. Key Differences Between HDB and Condo

HDB flats and private condos serve different buyer profiles. Here's a high-level comparison:

  • HDB Flat: Government-subsidised housing. Lower entry price. Eligible for CPF Housing Grants. 99-year lease. Resale levy applies if upgrading to another subsidised HDB. Cannot rent to foreigners.
  • Private Condo: Market-priced property. Higher entry price. Facilities (pool, gym, security). No resale levy. Can rent to foreigners. Freehold or 99-year leasehold options available.

2. Grants Available for HDB Buyers

First-time HDB buyers can tap into CPF Housing Grants that significantly reduce the purchase price. Key grants include:

  • Enhanced CPF Housing Grant (EHG): Up to $80,000 for families with a monthly household income of $9,000 or less
  • Family Grant: Up to $50,000 for first-timer families buying a resale flat near parents
  • Proximity Housing Grant (PHG): Up to $20,000 for living near parents
  • Singles Grant: Up to $40,000 for first-timer singles aged 35+

Combined, a qualifying couple can receive significant grants that cover a meaningful portion of downpayment needs.

Source: HDB — CPF Housing Grants (hdb.gov.sg). Verify current grant eligibility and amounts with HDB directly.

Key insight: If you're eligible for maximum grants, buying a resale HDB can be significantly more affordable than going straight to a private condo. This is a powerful argument for starting with HDB if you qualify.

3. Monthly Holding Costs — General Comparison

Beyond the purchase price, monthly costs matter a lot for cash flow. Condos typically have higher ongoing costs due to MCST maintenance fees, higher property tax, and larger mortgages. HDB flats have lower conservancy charges and generally smaller loan amounts.

4. Upgrade Path: The "HDB First" Strategy

A common strategy for young Singaporeans is the HDB First → Upgrade Later approach:

  1. First 5 years: Buy resale HDB with grants. Pay down mortgage. Build equity.
  2. After MOP: Sell HDB. Use proceeds + savings for condo downpayment.
  3. Long-term: Buy private condo as next step. No resale levy when upgrading from HDB to private property.

This path allows first-timers to benefit from grants and lower monthly payments initially, while building towards private property ownership later.

5. Quick Verdict

HDB may suit you if: You qualify for grants, need to keep monthly costs manageable, and plan to upgrade later.

Condo may suit you if: You have higher income and savings, value facilities and lifestyle amenities, and plan to stay long-term.
