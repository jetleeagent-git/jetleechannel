ABSD (Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty) in Singapore 2026 — Complete Guide

Published 22 June 2026 by Jet Lee, Senior Associate Group District Director, PropNex Realty

Quick summary: Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD) is a tax Singapore imposes on property buyers based on their residency status and number of properties owned. Since the April 2023 cooling measures raised rates sharply, buyers must carefully plan their property purchase order to avoid paying thousands in extra duties.

Current ABSD Rates (2026)

The following rates have been in effect since the 27 April 2023 cooling measures. As of mid-2026, no further changes have been announced.

Key point: The foreigner ABSD of 60% means a foreign buyer purchasing a $2M condo pays $1.2M in ABSD alone — on top of Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD). This has dramatically reduced foreign buyer demand and cooled the luxury segment.

What About Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD)?

ABSD is in addition to the standard Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD), which every property buyer pays. BSD rates in 2026 are:

Real-World Example: Buying a $1.5M Condo

Janice (SC, first property):

  • BSD: $34,600
  • ABSD: $0 (first home)
  • Total stamp duty: $34,600

Michael (SC, second property):

  • BSD: $34,600
  • ABSD: $300,000 (20%)
  • Total stamp duty: $334,600

The difference: $300,000 in extra tax — that's a 10X increase for buying a second property.

How ABSD Applies to Couples

When a married couple buys a property together, ABSD is based on the higher of the two profiles. For example:

  • SC + SC, no other properties: 0% ABSD (first home)
  • SC + SC, one already owns a property: 20% ABSD (second property applies to the couple)
  • SC + PR, no other properties: 5% ABSD (PR rate applies)
  • SC + Foreigner, no other properties: 60% ABSD (foreigner rate applies as higher profile)

This is why many mixed-nationality couples choose to buy under the SC name alone to avoid the higher ABSD.

ABSD Exemptions & Remission Paths

You can avoid or reduce ABSD in certain situations:

  1. Marital Decoupling: One spouse buys out the other's share to reset ABSD profile — but IRAS has tightened rules. Decoupling on a property purchased after May 2024 incurs ABSD on the acquiring spouse.
  2. Sale Within 6 Months: If you buy a second property and sell your first within 6 months, you can apply for ABSD remission (refund). Note: this is a remission, not an exemption — you pay first, then claim back.
  3. Development Remission: Housing developers can apply for remission on the 35% ABSD for qualifying projects, subject to completion and sale deadlines.
  4. Trust Arrangements: Buying property in a trust no longer avoids ABSD — transfers to trusts attract 65% ABSD since 2022.
Pro tip: The "6-month sale" remission is the most commonly used path for upgraders. If you sell your HDB within 6 months of exercising the Option to Purchase (OTP) on a condo, you can recover the 20% ABSD. This requires careful timing with your HDB agent.

Strategies to Minimise ABSD

  • Buy as first property: Always maximise your 0% first-property benefit. Don't buy a "cheap" second property before your dream home.
  • Choose the lower-ABSD spouse: For mixed SC/PR couples, consider buying under the SC name alone to enjoy 0% ABSD on the first home.
  • Timing the upgrade: If upgrading from HDB to condo, coordinate the sale completion and condo OTP exercise within the 6-month remission window.
  • Consider gift / CPF nomination: For property inheritance planning, trust-based avoidance is no longer viable. Plan ahead with a will instead.
