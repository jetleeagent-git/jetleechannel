Selling Your HDB Flat in 2026 — Step-by-Step Guide for Maximum Returns

Published 22 June 2026 by Jet Lee, Senior Associate Group District Director, PropNex Realty

Quick summary: Selling an HDB flat in 2026 requires careful planning — from meeting MOP requirements and understanding the new COV landscape to managing CPF refunds and timing the market. With resale prices up 54% since 2020, many sellers are sitting on significant gains. This guide walks you through every step.

Step 1: Confirm You Can Sell — MOP & Eligibility

Before listing your flat, confirm you've met the Minimum Occupation Period (MOP):

  • Standard MOP: 5 years from key collection or date of purchase (whichever is later)
  • Subsidised HDB: If you bought with a CPF Housing Grant, the MOP is still 5 years
  • DBSS / Design-Build-and-Sell Scheme: Also 5 years MOP

You can list your flat 3 months before MOP completion, but the sale can only be completed after the MOP date. If you're selling to upgrade to a private property, there's no waiting period after MOP.

Source: HDB — Resale Flat Selling (hdb.gov.sg)

Step 2: Know Your Flat's Market Value

Current market conditions (mid-2026):

  • HDB Resale Price Index: 203.4 (up 54% since 2020, but flat quarter-on-quarter)
  • COV Range: $15K–$35K in mature estates (buyers paying above valuation)
  • Demand: Strong for 4-room and 5-room flats near MRT stations

To estimate your selling price:

  1. Check recent transacted prices for similar units in your block (same floor level, renovation condition, facing)
  2. Get a HDB valuation — this costs ~$120 and takes 3–5 working days
  3. Factor in COV potential — if your flat is renovated, high floor, or in a popular estate, expect buyers to pay above valuation
Key point: Location plays a major role in selling price and COV potential. Flats in mature estates near MRT stations tend to attract higher offers and more buyer interest. Contact Jet Lee for recent transaction data specific to your estate.

Step 3: Prepare Your Flat for Sale

Well-presented flats generally attract more buyer interest and can sell faster. Consider the following preparation steps:

  • Declutter and deep clean — this costs almost nothing but makes the biggest difference
  • Fix visible issues — leaking taps, cracked tiles, peeling paint
  • Repaint neutral colours — white, cream, or light grey appeal to the widest audience
  • Consider professional home staging — it can help buyers visualise the space and may lead to a faster sale
  • Take professional photos — 90% of buyers start their search online
Tip: Good presentation can positively influence how much a buyer is willing to offer. A fresh coat of paint and clean, clutter-free spaces go a long way.

Step 6: CPF Refund & Resale Levy

When you sell your HDB, here's what happens to your money:

  • CPF Refund (Accrued Interest): You must refund to CPF the full amount you used (including accrued interest at 2.5% OA rate). This is not a penalty — the money goes back to your CPF account for future use.
  • Cash Proceeds: Whatever remains after repaying your outstanding loan and CPF refund is yours in cash.
  • Resale Levy: If you bought a subsidised HDB flat (with grant) and plan to buy another subsidised HDB, you pay a resale levy (ranges from $30K to $55K depending on flat type). If you're upgrading to private property, no resale levy applies.

Source: HDB — Resale Levy (hdb.gov.sg)
